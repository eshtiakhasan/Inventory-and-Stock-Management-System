# PRD Quality Review — Mojud (working name)

## Overall verdict
This is a disciplined PRD for a solo beginner build. It has a clear thesis ("trust through transparent, effortless accountability"), a strong glossary, explicit non-goals, numbered NFRs and SMs with counter-metrics, and an Assumptions Index that matches the inline tags. The risk sits in the stock-state engine, the part §4.4 calls "the heart of the system". Several rules break when physical reality runs ahead of the recorded state: count shortages larger than Available, damage found on Reserved stock, and offline actions refused at sync. Several Issue types also have no defined decision outcomes. Fix these and a few structural gaps (User–Warehouse assignment, password recovery, increment dependencies) before UX and epics. Without those fixes the builder will be inventing ledger rules while coding.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions: FR-2 "*Decided: v1 has no SMS one-time code*", FR-18 "*Decided for v1:* Unit cost = the cost from the latest Receipt", FR-23 one Warehouse per Dispatch order (with rationale in addendum §C). Trade-offs name what was given up: weighted-average costing deferred, photos deferred, notifications replaced by the inbox. The open items in §9 are genuinely open, and FR-30's damage-approval question is honestly flagged "open for validation".

What is missing is acknowledgement of the tensions the PRD creates for itself. The no-negative rule (FR-13) applied to offline actions (FR-44) means a physically completed dispatch or a real damage event can be *refused*. That is a significant trade-off of record integrity against floor truth, and the PRD does not name it. The three "more than expected" policies also differ without explanation.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Inconsistent overage policy with no rationale (§4.5 FR-22, §4.7 FR-28, §4.10 FR-36). Supplier over-delivery "is still recorded as Available". Transfer overage: "nothing extra is added until a Supervisor decides". Count surplus is added "only when the case is resolved". The builder will reasonably ask whether this is deliberate. *Fix:* either align them (hold all overages pending decision) or add one line per FR, or in addendum §C, explaining why supplier overage is trusted immediately.
- **[low]** §9 Q4 still open although FR-2 says decided (§9 Q4 vs FR-2, §10). "Is phone number + password acceptable…?" reads as unresolved, while §10 says "no longer an assumption". *Fix:* reword Q4 as a validation check on a decision ("Confirm owners accept no SMS code; revisit only if they refuse").

## Substance over theater — strong
The content is earned. The six roles in §2.1 each drive permissions in FR-4 and are not decorative personas. The UJs carry concrete numbers ("92 cartons counted, 100 ordered"; "Narayanganj 32, Gazipur 20"). The NFRs have thresholds (1 s search, 2 s save, 360 px, 99% uptime, 30-day backup retention, a 10-minute-demo usability bar), and §5 labels them provisional. There is no innovation theater. The Vision is specific to Bangladeshi FMCG distribution (khatas, taka, cartons and pieces). Only small traces of furniture remain.

### Findings
- **[low]** SM-3 is tautological (§8 SM-3). FR-11 and NFR-5 already make User, time and Reason mandatory, so "100% of Stock movements have a User, time and Reason" can only fail if the code is broken. It is a test, not a success metric. *Fix:* drop it, or move it to NFR-5 as an automated check.
- **[low]** Soft bounds in NFRs (§5 NFR-1, NFR-10). "typical low-cost Android phone" names no reference device, NFR-1 gives no percentile, and NFR-10 says "with no slowdown". *Fix:* name one reference device class (e.g., "2 GB RAM Android 10+"), state p90, and change NFR-10 to "NFR-1 targets still met at this scale".

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is explicit in §1 ("Recording stock has to be quicker than not recording it… gives the person who made it something back"). The features follow from it: blind counts, Unexplained state, the ranked inbox instead of notifications, "My record" (FR-51), and the next-task handoff in FR-25. MVP kind is problem-solving, and the scope logic matches. Counter-metrics exist and are well-targeted (SM-C2 "unknown" resolutions, SM-C3 suppressed damage flags). SM-7 ("one real distributor… 8 weeks in a row") is the right proof for this stakes level.

### Findings
- **[medium]** SM-1 is ambiguous (§8 SM-1). "at least 97% of counted lines match On hand after 3 months of use, compared with the first count" mixes an absolute target (97%) with a relative comparison ("compared with the first count"). It is unclear whether the target is ≥97% absolute, or an improvement over the first-count baseline. *Fix:* "≥97% of counted lines match On hand in counts taken in month 3; the first count's match rate is recorded as the baseline."
- **[low]** Some SMs measure activity or the wrong thing (§8 SM-2, SM-5, SM-C1). SM-2's "at least one action… every working week" is a very low adoption bar for a thesis about recording *every* movement. SM-5 is titled "Same-day damage capture" but measures *decision* time. SM-C1 ("at least weekly") is a floor target, not a counter-metric. *Fix:* consider measuring SM-2 as the share of Receipts and Dispatch orders recorded in Mojud, rename SM-5 "Damage decided within 24 h", and phrase SM-C1 as "track count frequency and coverage".

## Done-ness clarity — thin
Most FRs have testable consequences: FR-6 uniqueness rules, FR-10 "4th active Warehouse is refused", FR-14 idempotency, FR-21 "shows Products but not ordered or outstanding quantities", FR-47 preview-before-save. That makes them good story seeds.

The weakness is concentrated where it matters most: the transitions between Stock states, and what each Issue type lets a decider do. A builder implementing FR-36 literally will hit FR-13 refusals. The builder implementing FR-32 will find that "the allowed decision" is undefined for half the Issue types. Story creation will stall on these.

### Findings
- **[critical]** Count shortage taken "from Available" can break the no-negative rule (§4.10 FR-36, §4.4 FR-13, Glossary "On hand"). FR-36 compares the count with **On hand** (Available + Reserved + Damaged), but says "the missing quantity moves from Available to Unexplained". Take a Warehouse with 10 Available, 30 Reserved and a count 20 short of On hand: Available would become −10. FR-13's assumption says the shortage "does not create a negative balance" but gives no rule that makes this true. It also calls this "the only exception" when it is not one, since nothing goes negative. *Fix:* define the order in which states absorb a shortage (e.g., Available first, then Damaged, then Reserved, with any affected Dispatch order flagged as an Issue that ranks first per FR-31). Rewrite the FR-13 assumption as "Stock count shortages never go negative because [rule]". Also state whether offline count lines (FR-41) use device time or server time for "the moment the line was counted".
- **[high]** Damage on Reserved stock is not covered (§2.3 UJ-2, §4.8 FR-29/FR-30, §4.9 FR-31). In UJ-2 Karim flags a crushed carton *while picking a Dispatch order*. That carton is Reserved, yet the UJ and FR-29 both say it "moves from Available to Damaged". If Available is 0, FR-13 refuses the flag. If it is not 0, the books shift the wrong unit and the Dispatch order still reserves a crushed carton. FR-31's ranking "Issues affecting Reserved stock come first" depends on a path that no FR defines. *Fix:* let FR-29 take the quantity from Reserved when flagged from a pick (linked to the Dispatch order), and say what happens to the order: the reservation drops, or is refilled from Available if possible. On Reject, say whether the quantity returns to Reserved or Available.
- **[high]** Decision outcomes are undefined for most Issue types (§4.5 FR-22, §4.7 FR-28, §4.9 FR-32, §4.12 FR-44). FR-32 says "take the allowed decision (approve, reject, resolve)", but only Damage flags (FR-30), Transfer shortages (FR-28) and Discrepancy cases (FR-37) have defined outcomes with Stock-state effects. The following have none: the Shortfall Issue (does closing it change the PO? any movement?), over-delivery (accept, or return?), Transfer *overage* ("until a Supervisor decides" — decides what?), the 48-hour overdue Transfer Issue, and the Sync conflict. *Fix:* add a single table in §4.9 listing, for each Issue type, who decides, the allowed outcomes, and each outcome's Stock movement (or "none").
- **[high]** Sync conflicts have no resolution path, and the worker has no channel (§4.12 FR-44, FR-41, FR-31, §6). An offline dispatch confirmation refused at sync means goods physically left while the ledger still shows Reserved. A refused offline Damage flag means a real crushed carton is still Available. FR-44 creates "a Sync conflict Issue for a Supervisor" but gives the Supervisor no options. "the User who recorded the action is told about it" has no mechanism: §6 excludes notifications, and FR-31 shows the inbox only to Supervisors, Managers and Owners. *Fix:* define Sync conflict outcomes (e.g., "apply with adjusted quantity", "apply after Reversal of the conflicting movement", "discard with Reason"). State where the worker sees it (a banner in My record or the Offline queue status in FR-42). Name the integrity-vs-floor-truth trade-off explicitly.
- **[medium]** FR-29 contradicts NFR-2 on effort (§4.8 FR-29 vs §5 NFR-2). FR-29 says "one scan, one quantity entry and one tap"; NFR-2 says "flag damage… at most one scan and one tap". *Fix:* pick one. The likely answer is that quantity defaults to 1 Pack unit (or the scanned unit) and counts as a default.
- **[medium]** Unit cost scope is ambiguous (§3 "Unit cost", §4.4 FR-16/FR-18, §4.5 FR-20, FR-48). FR-18 says "a Unit cost for each Product" (business-wide), but FR-16 enters a Unit cost "per Product per Warehouse", which may differ. The Glossary defines Unit cost per Base unit, but FR-20's "unit purchase cost" does not say per carton or per piece. "current Unexplained value" (FR-48) does not say whether to use the stored movement cost or today's Unit cost. *Fix:* state that Unit cost is per Product, business-wide, per Base unit. Say PO cost may be entered per Pack unit and is converted. Say Unexplained and loss values use the Unit cost stored on the movement (FR-18 bullet 2).
- **[medium]** Reversal interactions are undefined (§4.4 FR-15). Several cases are not covered: reversing a movement that has an open Issue (a Damage flag, a count shortage in Unexplained), reversing a Write-off, and reversing a dispatch after goods left. *Fix:* add the rule "A movement linked to an open Issue cannot be reversed; decide the Issue instead", and say whether Write-offs and dispatches can be reversed.
- **[medium]** "Confirmed loss" means two different things (§4.7 FR-28 vs §4.10 FR-37). In FR-28, "confirmed loss" moves stock to Unexplained and *opens* a Discrepancy case. In FR-37, "Confirmed loss" is a Write-off *from* Unexplained. A Transfer loss therefore needs two "confirmed loss" decisions. *Fix:* rename the FR-28 outcome (e.g., "not found → investigate"), or let FR-28 write off directly.
- **[medium]** The 24-hour offline limit has no defined end state (§4.12 FR-41). "After that, the device asks the User to reconnect." It is unclear whether further recording is blocked, or whether queued actions older than 24 h are still sent. FR-44 says "No queued action is ever lost silently". *Fix:* "After 24 h offline, new actions are blocked until sync; queued actions are kept and sent on reconnect."
- **[low]** Stock state has no value for stock entering or leaving the Business (§3 "Stock state", FR-11). FR-11 requires from-state and to-state, but Receipts, dispatches and Write-offs have one side outside the Business, and the Glossary says state is "always exactly one of" five values. *Fix:* add a Glossary note that from/to may be "outside" (supplier, customer or loss).
- **[low]** Adjectives and undefined bounds (FR-10 "clear message", FR-38 "On supported phone browsers"). *Fix:* name the minimum supported browser (e.g., Chrome on Android, latest two versions), which also ties to §9 Q3.
- **[low]** Dispatch order and Transfer lifecycles are not defined (§4.6, §4.7). The PO has statuses (FR-20), but Dispatch orders and Transfers do not, although FR-24 ("not been dispatched"), FR-25 ("next open Dispatch order") and FR-28 ("not received within 48 hours", including partial receipt?) depend on them. *Fix:* list statuses for each, as FR-20 does.

## Scope honesty — adequate
§6 Non-Goals does real work (no batch/lot, no bins, no trust scores, no notifications, no native app). The §7.3 SHOULD/COULD lists are explicit, the 18 MUSTs are mapped one-to-one to FRs in §7.1, and the open-items density (7 Open Questions plus about 14 assumption tags) is fine for a learning project. Two omissions are silent, though, and a beginner would only find them in production.

### Findings
- **[high]** No password recovery, and no omission note (§4.1 FR-2/FR-3). Sign-in is phone + password with no SMS and no email. Nothing says how a User who forgets a password regains access. That matters most when the sole Owner forgets, because FR-3 lets only an Owner or Manager manage Users. FR-3's "temporary password" also has no forced change on first sign-in. *Fix:* add an FR stating that an Owner or Manager can reset another User's password to a new temporary one that must be changed at next sign-in. Add an explicit `[ASSUMPTION]` or non-goal for Owner self-recovery (e.g., "manual recovery by the Mojud operator in v1").
- **[medium]** Customer returns and supplier returns are neither in scope nor listed as non-goals (§4.6, §6, FR-30). Returns are routine in FMCG distribution. FR-30 mentions "return to Supplier" only as a possible future outcome, and customer returns after dispatch are not mentioned anywhere. Today the only path would be a Reversal (FR-15) or a fake Receipt. *Fix:* add a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` line to §6 with a suggested workaround, or an assumption saying how returns are recorded in v1.
- **[low]** Archive and deactivate checks ignore Unexplained and incoming stock (FR-7, FR-10). A Product with open Unexplained stock can be archived, and a Warehouse with stock In transit toward it can be deactivated. *Fix:* extend both checks to "no open Issues, Unexplained or incoming In transit".

## Downstream usability — adequate
This PRD is chain-top (UX → architecture → epics), so this dimension matters. On the strong side: a 34-term Glossary with Stock states defined precisely, contiguous IDs (FR-1–52, NFR-1–11, SM-1–7, SM-C1–3, UJ-1–7), "Realizes UJ-n" links that resolve, and an FR-to-MUST trace table. Against that, the permission model is not a single source of truth, a core entity relation is missing, and the build-increment table has dependency breaks that will mislead epic sequencing.

### Findings
- **[high]** No User–Warehouse assignment (§3 "User", FR-3, FR-26, FR-31, §9 Q5). FR-26 ("from their Warehouse"), FR-31 ("the Warehouses they work with"), FR-25 ("next open Dispatch order for that Warehouse") and UJ-1 ("Rahim… at the Narayanganj warehouse") all assume Users belong to Warehouses. FR-3 captures only name, phone, Role and password. *Fix:* add "assigned Warehouses (one or more)" to FR-3 and to the Glossary "User" entry. State the default (all Warehouses) and whether it limits actions or only filters views. This also answers Q5.
- **[high]** The build increment plan has forward dependencies (§7.2). The plan claims "Each increment should be usable and testable on its own", but:
  - Increment 2 creates Shortfall and over-delivery Issues (FR-22), and Increment 4 creates Transfer Issues (FR-28), yet the Issues inbox and decisions arrive in Increment 5 (FR-31–33).
  - FR-28's "confirmed loss" opens a Discrepancy case, which is built in Increment 6.
  - FR-27's sender ≠ receiver check cites FR-5, which is in Increment 5.

  *Fix:* move a minimal Issue record + list + decide (FR-31/FR-32 without ranking) and FR-5 into Increment 2. Alternatively, have FR-28 resolve Transfer losses without a Discrepancy case until Increment 6.
- **[medium]** The FR-4 permission table and the FR text drift apart (§4.1 FR-4 vs FR-6, FR-16, FR-19, FR-29, FR-33, FR-34, FR-46, FR-50, FR-51). Many FRs say "A Manager can…" and omit the Owner, while the table gives the Owner the same rights. FR-29 limits flagging to "Warehouse worker or Supervisor", but the table includes Owner and Manager. Several actions have no table row at all: start a Stock count, assign Issues, edit or cancel a Dispatch order, link a Barcode, import Excel, view another User's record. FR-50 limits the low-stock list to four Roles, but the table's "View stock" covers all six. *Fix:* make FR-4 the single source by adding the missing rows, and replace role names in FR text with "a User whose Role allows [row]". Alternatively, state once that the Owner can do everything a Manager can.
- **[medium]** UJs contradict FRs (§2.3 UJ-4, UJ-6, UJ-1). In UJ-4, Tanvir "creates a Dispatch order for 50 from both warehouses", which contradicts FR-23 ("One Dispatch order uses one Warehouse"). UJ-6 sums "৳2,300 unexplained" into "৳18,400 lost" for the month, while FR-48 reports losses by Reason and treats Unexplained as a separate *current* balance; the UJ also omits "confirmed loss". In UJ-1 the Shortfall opens "On submit", while FR-22 opens it only "if… the User marks the delivery as complete". UX will design from the UJs. *Fix:* in UJ-4, have Tanvir create two orders. Align the UJ-6 figures with FR-48's categories. Add "marks delivery complete" to UJ-1.
- **[medium]** There is no defined list of Reason codes (§3 "Reason", FR-11, FR-29, FR-37, FR-45, FR-48). Reasons are scattered: "received", "dispatched", "opening balance", "damaged/expired/other", five confirmed-loss reasons, and the Glossary's "count shortage" (which no FR uses). FR-48 groups losses as "damaged, expired, other, confirmed loss", and FR-45 requires Reasons to be translated. *Fix:* add one Reason table (code, used by which FR, and loss category for FR-48).

## Shape fit — strong
This is a multi-role B2B operational tool with meaningful floor UX, so UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing, and seven is the right density. The "hobby / solo" rigor-light allowance is used well: plain language, small FRs, no `[NOTE FOR PM]` ceremony, and technical options pushed to the addendum and kept out of requirements. Nothing here is over-formalized. No findings.

## Mechanical notes
- **Broken cross-references:**
  - FR-16 says Excel import is in "(§4.13)", but it is §4.14; §4.13 is Language.
  - FR-27 cites "(FR-5)" for sender ≠ receiver, but FR-5 governs Issue decisions, not Transfer receipt. Either broaden FR-5 or state the FR-27 rule on its own.
- **Glossary drift:**
  - "Discrepancy case" is defined as "opened when a count does not match On hand", but FR-28 also opens one from a Transfer loss.
  - "Blind receiving" (§4.5 heading, FR-21) and "Blind count" (Glossary) are used as near-synonyms.
  - UJ-7's "found it, recount OK" differs from FR-37's "Found".
  - The FR-28 Transfer-difference Issue has no Glossary name, while the equivalent receiving difference is a "Shortfall".
  - Several terms are used but not defined: "owner view" (also a FR-4 row), "value at risk" (only defined inline in FR-31), "counter", "pick", "loss" (vs Write-off), "over-delivery Issue", "stock ladder".
  - FR-13's "at any Warehouse" sits awkwardly with In transit, which per the Glossary is not at a Warehouse. Say In transit is held per Transfer.
- **Issue types:** the Glossary lists Issue types with "such as". An enumerated list would anchor the decision table recommended above.
- **ID continuity:** FR-1–52, NFR-1–11, SM-1–7, SM-C1–3 and UJ-1–7 are contiguous and unique. The §7.1 and §7.2 tables each cover all 52 FRs.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** complete. Every inline `[ASSUMPTION]` (FR-2, FR-5, FR-13, FR-22, FR-23, FR-28 ×2, FR-30, FR-31, FR-36, FR-41, FR-45, §5, §7.2, §8) appears in §10, and every §10 entry appears inline. The index order is slightly off (FR-30 is listed before FR-23).
- **UJ protagonists:** UJ-5 ("A Warehouse worker at Gazipur") and UJ-7 ("The Manager") have no named persona, unlike the others. Naming them is a minor fix.
- **Required sections:** all present for the stakes (Vision, Users and JTBD, UJs, Glossary, FRs, NFRs, Non-Goals, Scope, SMs with counter-metrics, Open Questions, Assumptions Index).
